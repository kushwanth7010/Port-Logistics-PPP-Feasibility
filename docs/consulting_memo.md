# Decision memo | Hypothetical port/logistics PPP

**Decision requested:** Should the sponsoring authority proceed from concept to detailed diligence on a multi-purpose port/logistics terminal?

**Recommendation:** Advance to detailed diligence only; do not approve investment or announce a PPP bid on this model alone. The model is illustrative, uses invented inputs, and requires cargo, tariff, capex, financing, environmental and stakeholder validation.

## Situation and issue tree
- **Market:** What commodity flows are addressable? Which shippers are anchor customers? Who are the competing ports?
- **Technical:** Can capacity, berths, evacuation infrastructure, land, utilities and environmental approvals support forecast throughput?
- **Economics:** What are capex, operating costs, tariff assumptions, project NPV and project IRR?
- **PPP structure:** Who carries traffic, construction, environmental, operating, interest-rate, and regulatory risks?
- **Implementation:** When can approvals, procurement, construction and commissioning realistically occur?

## Model method
Annual captured demand = addressable market × capture rate, constrained by installed terminal capacity.

Revenue = processed tonnes × realized tariff. EBITDA = revenue − variable opex − fixed opex. Tax = 25% × max(EBITDA − straight-line depreciation, 0). Project free cash flow = EBITDA − cash tax − working-capital change + any residual value. Year 0 includes initial project capex. Project IRR is the IRR on unlevered project cash flows. Project NPV discounts those cash flows at a stated 12% case hurdle rate, which is **an assumption, not an observed project WACC**.

This simplified model omits debt, interest, inflation, concession fees, replacement capex, interest tax shields, detailed tax-loss carryforwards, GST and construction-phased expenditures. **Project IRR is not equity IRR**; a positive project NPV does not by itself establish PPP suitability, value for money or environmental acceptability.

## Decision gates
1. Obtain independent origin–destination and commodity-wise demand studies, with shipper validation.
2. Verify technical design, site availability, customs/logistics requirements and evacuation capacity.
3. Validate capex, operating costs, tariffs and revenue-sharing proposals.
4. Run financing and lender cases with debt-service coverage, completion-delay and traffic stress tests.
5. Prepare stakeholder map and preliminary PPP risk register.
6. Proceed to formal procurement only when project appraisal and required approvals are complete.

## Risk register
| Risk | Preliminary bearer (to negotiate) | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Land acquisition and statutory permissions | Usually public authority where it controls land/permits | Milestone-based land handover and clear approval conditions |
| Construction cost and schedule | Generally private EPC/concessionaire, subject to allocated relief | Fixed-price contracts, contingencies, independent engineer |
| Traffic / cargo demand | Depending on concession model; often private for usage-based PPP | Anchor-customer studies, phased investment, realistic tariff testing |
| Tariff / regulatory changes | Allocated contractually | Explicit change-in-law and tariff-adjustment clauses |
| Operational efficiency | Private operator | Measurable uptime and throughput KPIs |
| Environmental / community impacts | Shared depending on cause and scope | Baseline studies, compliance plan, stakeholder consultation |

*This allocation is illustrative, not a legal opinion or an assertion of one mandatory Indian PPP form.*
